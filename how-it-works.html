YouthCraft · The operating model

One thoughtful baseline.
A lighter daily rhythm.

YouthCraft is designed around two connected data layers: a one-time onboarding profile that establishes a starting point, and a recurring layer that can capture many measures automatically—with confirmation or manual entry when needed. Together, they support a changing view of habits, health signals, Youth Score and LIFE DAYS.

01 / The user journey

How the two layers work together

Onboarding is the context-setting layer; recurring capture is the continuity layer. They are related, but they do different jobs.

01

Build your profile

Answer relevant questions about demographics, biometrics, lifestyle, substances, medical history, preventive care, function and preferences.

02

Set the baseline

Applicable responses are mapped to domain-level measures, scoring lookups and any reviewed safety-trigger rules.

03

Connect or log

Recurring measures arrive from approved integrations where available, from confirmed document extraction, or from quick manual input.

04

Review and adjust

Daily feedback and longer time windows show patterns, suggest one next action, and help users revise goals over time.

Important distinction: “mostly automatic” is the intended product experience, not a claim that every metric is available automatically on every device. Availability depends on integration permissions, the source app, device, metric and user consent. Some information will still need confirmation or manual entry.
02 / Layer A

One-time onboarding: up to 107 questions

The onboarding profile establishes the user's starting context. The concept paper describes staged completion—typically designed for a 5–10 minute experience, with the option to resume later—and age- or cohort-relevant question blocks.

Baseline inputs

Six major domains

Demographics & biometricsLifestyle & daily habitsSubstance useMedical history & clinical markersAge-specific function & preventionPreferences & engagement

Question applicability may differ by life stage. The profile can include measurements such as age, height, weight and waist-related measures; behaviour patterns; reported conditions; selected clinical readings; and functional or preventive items.

What each answer can do

Map responses to a defined rule

In the framework, each item can be associated with a response option, a raw score or category, a domain, an instrument or guideline reference, cohort applicability, a proposed time-delta lookup, and optional medical-trigger flags.

Preferences—such as mission style, notification frequency or willingness to connect labs—are intended to personalise the experience and are not supposed to distort biological-age calculations.

What onboarding establishes

📍

Starting Youth Score

A baseline score out of 100 is described in the concept paper, with domain-level scores to make the overall signal easier to interpret.

🧬

Lifestyle-based age view

A model-derived biological-age proxy combines chronological age with domain-level time deltas, subject to defined and validated scoring rules.

🛡️

Safety and relevance

Potentially important responses can route to a reviewed trigger workflow, while age- or cohort-specific logic can determine which questions apply.

04 / Scoring engine

From inputs to interpretable outputs

The proposed engine converts applicable responses and recurring measures into domain contributions and time-based deltas. A key implementation principle is that every displayed result should be traceable to an input, a rule and a time window.

1. Validate input

Identify source, timestamp, units, range and whether confirmation is required.

2. Map to a band

Match the response or reading to the defined category, threshold or option lookup.

3. Apply scoring rule

Use the relevant domain weight, year-delta or recurring contribution when specified.

4. Check triggers

Evaluate separately governed safety rules and route to appropriate reviewed messaging.

5. Aggregate

Summarise daily contributions into weekly, monthly, quarterly and annual views.

Onboarding baseline

Baseline age view

Chronological age ± aggregated domain year-deltas
= lifestyle-informed biological-age proxy

This is the concept-level model description, not a clinical epigenetic clock. The exact aggregation, caps, cohort adjustments and calibrated coefficients must be explicitly specified before the result can be interpreted as a calibrated estimate.

Recurring update

Daily LIFE DAYS

Daily gains − daily penalties
± eligible trigger effects
+ optional mission / streak bonuses

The concept paper describes recurring metrics as fractional time-deltas translated into LIFE DAYS. A sustained pattern may be damped into the baseline over longer periods so the longer-term view does not swing with every individual day.

Conversion convention in the framework: 1 biological year is represented as 365 LIFE DAYS; therefore, a specified year delta can be converted to LIFE DAYS by multiplying by 365. This is a motivational model convention, not a literal prediction of lifespan gained or lost.

One scoring language, several time horizons

Higher horizons should summarise the same underlying daily records rather than invent new science or apply unrelated scoring logic.

Daily

Immediate metric contributions, net LIFE DAYS, mission feedback and any reviewed trigger messages.

Weekly

The main coaching window: progress toward the desired score or LIFE DAYS target, with a clear next action.

Monthly / quarterly

Smooth day-to-day noise, show domain trends, recurring patterns and whether support may be useful.

Annual

Reflect on total LIFE DAYS, score change, long-term trends, completed missions and the next baseline goal.

05 / Rules and guardrails

Traceability matters more than a polished number

For this concept to become a dependable product, the scoring engine needs a transparent rule catalogue and careful validation. The source framework identifies the intended structure, but it does not supply every operational coefficient.

🧾

What should be traceable

  • Original input and capture source
  • Question / metric ID and domain
  • Response band, threshold and units
  • Applied weight or year-delta lookup
  • Date, missing-data handling and confidence
  • Score contribution and reason for any change
🔐

What should be governed

  • Consent, source permissions and data correction
  • Duplicate, stale, implausible or conflicting readings
  • Age/cohort applicability, caps and damping
  • Clinically reviewed trigger thresholds and escalation paths
  • Validation, versioning and audit trail for scoring changes
  • Clear explanation that outputs are not a diagnosis
Scoring status: the science framework includes blank or unspecified YouthCraft year-delta values and some unfinished scoring entries. This page therefore explains the intended architecture and conversion convention, but does not invent missing weights, thresholds, coefficients or clinical cut-offs. Those values need to be completed, reviewed and validated before production scoring.
Safety status: onboarding and recurring inputs may identify conditions for a support workflow, but a trigger should not be treated as a diagnosis. Each trigger requires an approved threshold, clinical owner, user-facing language, destination, privacy controls and a process for false positives and missed cases.
