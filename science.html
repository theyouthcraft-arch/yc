Evidence & methodology

Science you can understand. Boundaries you can trust.

YouthCraft turns research on health risks into Life-days, a simple way to see what is at stake and what you can win back. This page shows how the numbers are built, how strong the evidence is, and what should never be read into them.

What it is, and what it isn’t

A motivational model,
not a medical test.

✓

Designed to support

  • Awareness: seeing where lifestyle, biometrics and history may matter.
  • Action: breaking a goal into small, trackable steps.
  • Consistency: feedback, streaks and Life-days you can claim.
  • Conversations: patterns worth discussing with a qualified professional.
!

Not designed to

  • Diagnose, treat or replace professional care.
  • Predict how long any individual will live.
  • Measure “biological age” in cells. Life-days are a modelled, motivational translation.
  • Treat a cited study as proof that YouthCraft’s exact Life-day values are right.
A paper can support a risk factor (for example, blood pressure and vascular mortality). It does not validate the specific Life-day values YouthCraft attaches to each answer. Those values are model parameters, described below, and they are still being refined.

How a Life-day figure is built

Five steps, all visible.

Worked example: Q001, usual systolic blood pressure, answer “160 mmHg or above”.

Potential loss

Each question has a maximum Life-days value (60 to 3,500 across the bank); each answer takes a share of it. Q001: 450.

Recoverable

A judged share of that loss can be modified by action, from 0% to 95% by question. Q001: 60%, so 270.

Weighted

An effective weight = residual weight × age modifier × sex modifier. Q001: 0.75 × 1.236 × 1.0 = 0.927, so 250.3.

Adherence

Multiplied by an adherence factor, currently 0.50, because few people sustain every action. Q001: 125.1.

Cap

Totals are capped at 9,125 Life-days (25 years) to keep results plausible.

Life-years are shown as Life-days ÷ 365.25. For each question, the reclaimable Life-days are shared across its action steps (equally by default) and claimed step by step.

Limits we state up front

What is still prototype.

  • Hazard ratios are scaled, not extracted. The current values were derived by scaling each option’s share of maximum Life-days against a domain-level maximum risk. They are illustrative and are meant to be replaced with point estimates extracted from the primary studies.
  • Overlap weights are judgements. Residual weights express how strongly related factors overlap. They are editable and need epidemiological review.
  • Recoverable shares are judgements. “How much of a loss is modifiable” is a rough estimate, to be calibrated against published intervention evidence.
  • Age and sex effects are simplified. Hazard ratios are assumed to be constant across adult ages; many vary with age.
  • Mission thresholds are defaults. For example, 21 positive days in 30 for a daily habit to count as done.
Screening-style questions and red-flag alerts are prompts for follow-up, not diagnoses. If you have symptoms or concerns, speak to a qualified professional. In an emergency, call your local emergency number.
