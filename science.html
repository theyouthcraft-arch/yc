YouthCraft brings together health measures, evidence from population studies, and practical behaviour-change design. This page explains what the framework measures, how its proposed scoring model is organised, and what should not be inferred from its outputs.

Evidence-informed inputs

Where applicable, the framework references established questionnaires, clinical guidance and epidemiological studies. A cited instrument does not automatically validate YouthCraft's combined score.

01 / The foundations

What YouthCraft is—and what it is not

The source framework describes a structured, multi-domain questionnaire and recurring measures intended to turn health-related information into understandable signals and supportive actions.

Designed to support

Awareness, reflection and action

  • Organise lifestyle, biometric, medical-history and age-specific information.
  • Highlight patterns that may be worth discussing with a qualified professional.
  • Help people choose practical, achievable health missions.
  • Track selected behaviours and measures over time.
Not a clinical instrument

Important limits

  • Not a diagnosis, medical device claim, or substitute for professional advice.
  • A “biological age” output is a model-derived proxy, not a direct measurement of cellular age.
  • “LIFE DAYS” are a motivational translation of model output, not literal days gained or lost.
  • Risk associations from population studies do not establish an individual's personal lifespan.
Interpretation principle: distinguish the evidence for an individual measure (for example, blood pressure or a validated screening questionnaire) from the evidence needed to validate YouthCraft's combined scoring algorithm. They are not the same thing.

02 / Measurement map

Six connected domains

The source document groups onboarding fields into six domains. The cards below summarise the scope and representative instruments named in the document; they are not a complete question-by-question specification.

🧍
C01–C09 / C05b noted in engine

1. Demographics & biometrics

Chronological baseline, height, weight/BMI, waist-to-height ratio, living status, education and occupation.

Why it matters

Provides baseline context and anthropometric indicators. BMI and waist-to-height ratio describe different aspects of body composition and should be interpreted in context.

References named: WHO life tables; Ashwell waist-to-height ratio work; social determinants of health framework.

03 / Model architecture

How the scoring engine is intended to work

The document proposes a conversion layer that maps selected risk metrics into time-based deltas, with cohort multipliers intended to reduce double counting across overlapping factors. The details below distinguish the documented design intent from items that still require specification.

Documented convention

LIFE DAYS conversion

1 year = 365 LIFE DAYS

The framework states that a model-derived year delta is translated into LIFE DAYS using a 365-day multiplier. This is a presentation convention, not evidence that a behaviour literally adds or subtracts that number of days from an individual's life.

Proposed model component

Risk-to-time mapping

The source describes relative-risk evidence from prospective cohorts, adjusted by age-band cohort multipliers to reduce overlapping effects. The final coefficients, age-band definitions and validation evidence must be available before the resulting output can be interpreted as calibrated.

04 / Safety & escalation

Medical triggers are safety prompts—not diagnoses

The source document describes 20 proposed medical-trigger workflows intended to identify responses or measurements that may warrant specialised follow-up. A trigger should lead to clear, proportionate guidance and human care pathways—not an automated diagnosis.

🫶

Mood and mental health

PHQ-2 or GDS-5 screening responses may prompt supportive language and a pathway to qualified mental-health care. Crisis support should be available where relevant, without relying solely on an algorithmic score.

🌙

Sleep and substance use

Persistent sleep concerns, tobacco use, potentially risky alcohol patterns or recreational substance responses may prompt evidence-informed resources and professional support options.

❤️

Cardiometabolic markers

Blood pressure, glucose, lipid, inflammation, liver or kidney markers may need repeat measurement or clinician interpretation. The app should communicate urgency only according to reviewed clinical protocols.

🧠

Cognition, falls and frailty

Memory-screen concerns, fall history, gait or grip findings, and sarcopenia or frailty screens may prompt assessment by suitable healthcare professionals.

What needs to be true before a numerical score is presented as validated?

Model transparency

  • Document every input, unit, missing-data rule and transformation.
  • Publish the complete coefficients, age adjustments, caps and interactions.
  • Define how correlated or overlapping risk factors are handled.
  • Separate population risk associations from individual prediction.

Validation and governance

  • Test calibration and performance in representative external populations.
  • Evaluate age, sex, socioeconomic and other subgroup performance.
  • Document uncertainty, limitations, intended use and version history.
  • Have qualified clinical and statistical reviewers approve trigger logic and claims.
Publication standard: cite the original study or official instrument, state the population and year, explain whether a claim is association or causation, and label YouthCraft-specific scoring as proposed until it has been independently validated.
