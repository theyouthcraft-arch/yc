YouthCraft Scorecard

The YouthCraft scorecard concept translates health-related measures, habits and risk factors into understandable progress signals. The proposed model uses Biological Year Deltas (Δ) and LIFE DAYS as motivational representations, connecting awareness to practical daily actions and appropriate clinical follow-up.

01 / Executive summary

From health information to an actionable scorecard

The framework describes three connected data components: a question bank, an option lookup table and a scoring engine. Together, these are intended to organise answers, map specified options to model impacts, and present progress in an accessible way.

Question Bank

Question_Bank_Full

Stores questions identified in the framework and links them to relevant measures or screening instruments where specified.

Option Lookup

Option_Lookup

Maps a question response to its configured impact fields, such as Year_Delta and Life_Days, where values are actually defined.

Scoring Core

Scoring_Engine

Evaluates answer selections keyed by QID and option, then aggregates the configured results for scorecard display.

Important: examples of Year_Delta values in the supplied concept are illustrative, not evidence of validated individual life-expectancy changes. The framework's combined scoring algorithm, coefficients and outputs require specification and validation before being presented as calibrated biological-age or lifespan estimates.
02 / Technical architecture

How the proposed score calculation is organised

The supplied description uses the following conventions. The formulas are presented here as framework logic, not as a clinically validated prediction model.

Proposed net biological-age calculation

Net Biological Age = Chronological Age − Σ(Year_Delta values)

Proposed conversion convention

LIFE DAYS Balance = Total Year_Delta × 365
Year_Delta (ΔY)

Model impact expressed in years

Each configured response may carry a positive or negative delta under the proposed lookup design. The meaning and sign convention must be defined consistently in the implementation, including how beneficial and risk-associated factors are handled.

Life_Days

A presentation currency

The source framework defines 1 model-derived year as 365 LIFE DAYS. This is a gamification conversion convention; it does not mean the system can measure literal days gained or lost for an individual.

Implementation rule: calculate only from explicit, versioned values and documented formulas. Do not fill missing coefficients, thresholds, confidence intervals or option deltas with invented numbers. Explain which inputs contributed to any displayed result.
03 / Gamifying recapture

Make progress visible—without promising lifespan gains

The gamification concept reframes a potentially modifiable risk factor as an opportunity to work on a supportive habit or seek care. Rewards should recognise actions and consistency rather than imply a verified biological or survival outcome.

05 / Medical trigger circuit breakers

Safety pathways before gamified rewards

The supplied concept proposes medical triggers that can interrupt the normal gamification flow when a response or measurement may need professional attention. A trigger should provide proportionate, clinically reviewed guidance.

🌙

MT-SLEEP

Frequent sleep or insomnia concerns may prompt information about further sleep assessment or a sleep-clinic pathway, depending on the reviewed rules.

🚭

MT-TOBACCO

Current tobacco use may prompt cessation resources and behavioural coaching options.

🩸

MT-GLU

Glucose or HbA1c results meeting configured clinical criteria may prompt confirmatory review and an appropriate healthcare pathway. Units, measurement conditions and escalation logic must be explicit.

🦵

MT-FRAIL

A SARC-F or frailty-screen result meeting configured criteria may prompt assessment for muscle function, mobility and rehabilitation needs.

Clinical governance requirement: before launch, each trigger should have a clinical owner, defined input mapping and units, reviewed threshold and severity logic, user-facing copy, referral destination, consent/privacy rules, false-positive and missed-case handling, and emergency instructions where relevant. Do not automate clinical escalation solely from an unvalidated composite score.
06 / Progress tracking

What the scorecard can communicate

A responsible scorecard can separate three different things: measured data, model-derived estimates and actions completed by the user.

Scorecard elementWhat it representsSuggested communication
Measured valuesInputs such as reported habits, questionnaire responses or clinical measurements.Show the value, date, source and units where applicable.
Model-derived deltasCalculated outputs based on configured scoring logic.Show only when formulas and coefficients are defined; label model status, uncertainty and limitations.
Habit progressCompletion of a user-selected daily or weekly action.Celebrate participation and consistency without translating each action into a guaranteed lifespan gain.
Clinical triggersA reviewed rule indicating that follow-up may be appropriate.Explain the reason for the prompt and the next step; do not present it as a diagnosis.
Recommended product principle: reward what the user can control—learning, habit practice, check-ins and appropriate follow-through—while keeping medical interpretation transparent and appropriately qualified.
