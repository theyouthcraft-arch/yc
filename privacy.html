1. Core Operating Principles

YouthCraft may handle sensitive personal health information, lifestyle habits, and physiological indicators. The following principles describe the intended operating posture and should be read alongside the actual features and safeguards available in the released service.

Explicit permission

Choose which devices, apps, and data categories connect. Consent should be granular and revocable.

Data minimisation

Collect only the information needed for features you choose, such as scores, trends, missions, and requested reminders.

Security by design

Protect personal data with appropriate encryption, access controls, and restricted access, based on safeguards actually deployed.

Access & portability

Provide practical ways to access, correct, export, and delete personal information where supported by the service.

No sale of health information

YouthCraft's stated policy is not to sell, rent, or trade identifiable health information to advertisers or data brokers.

User-initiated action

External referrals or transfers should happen only after an affirmative action and confirmation from the user.

2. Important Disclaimers & Scope

Important medical notice
YouthCraft is intended as a lifestyle engagement and personal wellness companion. It is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, mitigate, or prevent disease, and it does not replace professional medical advice or emergency services. Scores, domain metrics, biological-age views, and LIFE DAYS are algorithmically derived motivational indicators—not clinical diagnostic tools or literal predictions of lifespan.

This policy describes the intended handling of data associated with YouthCraft's features. Applicable legal duties depend on where the user is located, how the service operates, the entities involved, and the data and purposes at issue.

⚠ Before publishing as a binding policy

Confirm that all described practices match the live product, infrastructure, vendors, integrations, retention/deletion processes, and user controls. In particular, do not state that TLS 1.3, AES-256, 24-hour deletion, 30-day backup deletion, or any integration is operational unless it has been verified. Have qualified privacy counsel review applicability of GDPR, HIPAA, and state or regional health-privacy laws. HIPAA does not automatically apply to every wellness app.

A. Personal & baseline profile

  • Demographics & biometrics: age, sex, height, weight, and baseline markers.
  • Lifestyle & daily habits: sleep habits, activity levels, and diet-quality indicators.
  • Substance use: alcohol and tobacco screening responses.
  • Medical history & markers: self-reported health conditions and clinical-marker bands, where included.
  • Age-specific function & prevention: age-appropriate function checks and preventive-screening information.
  • Preferences & engagement: goals, reminders, and notification preferences.

Purpose: establish an initial Youth Score (0–100), domain views, and a lifestyle-informed biological-age proxy, subject to the scoring model and its validation.

B. Recurring daily & event metrics

  • Sleep & activity: sleep duration or quality, steps, and active minutes.
  • Nutrition & routines: meal or habit logs and daily mission completion.
  • Health measures: user-entered or connected metabolic readings, heart rate, medication logs, illness, or event data where the product supports them.

Purpose: track patterns and consistency, update daily feedback and LIFE DAYS displays, and suggest manageable missions. Not every measure is automatically available; some require user entry or confirmation.

C. Connected wearables & health apps (optional)

Potential integrations may include Apple HealthKit, Android Health Connect, Garmin, Fitbit, or other authorized services. Data access should be limited to the categories the user explicitly approves. Availability depends on the released product, device, operating system, and integration permissions.

4. Consent Architecture & Management

YouthCraft's intended approach is a granular, just-in-time consent model. Users should be able to make separate choices about data categories, connected services, and sharing features.

1. ChooseSelect a feature or data category
2. UnderstandSee purpose and data requested
3. AuthoriseGrant permission explicitly
4. ManageReview, revoke, export, or delete

Categorical permissions

Where supported, permissions can be separated by category—for example, sleep data, activity metrics, or metabolic readings—so that a user may enable one category without enabling others.

Connected device revocation

The intended control path is Settings → Connected Apps. Disconnecting a service should stop future syncing. At disconnection, users should be told whether previously synced data will be retained or deleted, and offered a choice where feasible and appropriate.

Permission names and settings paths above describe the intended experience and should be updated if the released interface differs.

5. Social & Challenge Sharing

YouthCraft's Discover–Craft–Play–Share concept is designed to support motivation without exposing a full health profile.

  • Private by default: no public sharing unless the user actively opts in.
  • Score cards & achievements: export or share on a case-by-case basis.
  • Private challenges: disclose only selected aliases and specific challenge metrics, such as step counts, rather than raw biometrics or a full health profile.
  • Choice and withdrawal: users should be able to review sharing choices and stop future sharing where the feature permits.

6. Partner Routing & Wellness Care

YouthCraft may present options for external support such as sleep coaching, smoking-cessation programmes, telehealth, or fall-prevention services.

  • Action-gated transfer: personal information should not be sent to a partner automatically. A transfer should occur only after the user selects a referral action and confirms what information will be shared and with whom.
  • Clear partner boundaries: explain whether the partner is independent, what its own privacy terms are, and what happens after leaving YouthCraft.
  • Not emergency care: partner services offered through YouthCraft are not emergency medical services. In an emergency, contact local emergency services.

Proposed retention schedule

  • Active accounts: retain data while the account is active for historical trends and multi-year progress, subject to user choices and applicable law.
  • Inactive accounts: the proposed policy flags accounts inactive for 24 consecutive months for deletion after prior notification.
  • Deletion timing: the policy brief specifies removal from live databases within 24 hours and cold backups within 30 days after account deletion. These timelines must be verified against actual systems, backups, legal holds, and vendor arrangements before being represented as a guarantee.

8. Your Data Rights & Self-Service Tools

You should be able to exercise applicable rights over your personal information. The exact rights and response timelines vary by jurisdiction and circumstances.

  1. Access & portability: request a copy of your data. The proposed product export includes onboarding fields, recurring metrics, mission history, and score logs in JSON or CSV, if those export tools are available.
  2. Correction: correct profile details and revise onboarding or daily inputs where supported, so records reflect your updated information.
  3. Erasure: request account deletion through the available account controls or privacy contact. Explain any exceptions, backup delays, and data that must be retained by law.
  4. Consent withdrawal: revoke permissions for connected apps, categories, or optional sharing. Withdrawal does not necessarily undo processing that occurred before withdrawal.
  5. Questions or complaints: contact the privacy team using the details below and follow any applicable supervisory-authority or regulator process.

Proposed self-service paths in the product brief: Settings → Export My Data and Settings → Delete Account. Confirm the paths and capabilities in the live app before publishing.

9. Ethical Data Standard & Algorithm Transparency

  • Evidence-linked scoring: scoring concepts may draw on established instruments and guidelines for sleep, physical activity, mood screening, alcohol use, and other health-related domains.
  • Traceable logic: option-level mapping and documented scoring rules should make score contributions explainable rather than opaque.
  • Progress without shame: feedback should encourage consistency, recovery, and manageable next steps rather than punitive messages for imperfect days.
  • Transparent limits: Youth Score, domain scores, biological-age views, and LIFE DAYS are model-derived motivational indicators, not clinical diagnoses or literal life-expectancy calculations.
  • Responsible change management: changes to scoring weights, bands, thresholds, or data use should be documented, tested, and communicated where material.

The scoring framework and data processing should be reviewed for reliability, bias, age-appropriate handling, and foreseeable misuse. Do not imply that an instrument's presence alone validates the complete composite algorithm.
